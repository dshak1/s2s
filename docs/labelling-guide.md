# Labelling guide

For educators, native speakers, and anyone else judging content at `/label`.

## Why you are doing this

The app can already measure that a question is hard. It counts how many kids got
it right and how long they hesitated. What it cannot do is tell us **why** — that
the recording says the word oddly, that the wrong answers give the game away,
that the English gloss is fine but nobody actually says it that way.

That judgement is the part of this dataset that cannot be scraped or generated.
It is the reason the whole thing is worth building.

## The verdict

Pick one:

- **Good question** — you would put this in front of a class tomorrow.
- **Not sure** — you have a doubt you can't pin down. Say what nags you.
- **Something's wrong** — a reason is required. A rejection nobody can act on
  isn't data.

## The five axes, 1–5

| Axis | 1 means | 5 means |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | You can't tell what's being asked | Obvious on sight |
| **Difficulty** | Far too easy for a 5–12 year old | Far too hard |
| **Culturally right** | Wrong, dated, or odd in Kazakh | Exactly how it's said |
| **Answer correct** | The marked answer is wrong | The marked answer is right |
| **Wrong options** | Giveaways, or unfairly close | Plausible and fair |

Note that **Difficulty is not a quality score.** A 5 is not bad — some items
should be hard. Rate what it *is*, not whether you like it. Everything else is a
quality score where 5 is better.

3 is the honest default. Don't inflate.

## Writing the reason

The reason is read by whoever fixes the question. Useful reasons say what is
wrong **and what it should be instead**:

> "The clip for Ұ sounds like Ү. Needs re-recording with the short back vowel."

> "Options are Әке / Ана / Ата — all family words, but a 6-year-old will just
> pick the longest. Mix in one non-family word."

> "'Bauyr' is glossed 'Sibling' but it's used for a younger relative or a close
> friend. 'Younger brother / close friend' is closer."

Not useful: "bad", "wrong", "doesn't work".

**Say it instead of typing it.** The mic button records, saves the audio, and
transcribes it into the box. Talking through a pronunciation problem is faster
and more precise than writing it, and the recording is kept either way.

## What the queue shows you first

1. Questions a generator flagged as uncertain, with the reason it was unsure.
2. Questions real learners get wrong almost always, or right almost always.
3. Questions learners have answered that nobody has judged.
4. Everything else, so it never runs dry.

Some items in the queue have a known correct judgement and are used to check how
consistently the group rates. They look like any other item. Don't think about
it; just rate honestly.

## Ground rules

- One label per person per item. Coming back to an item updates your label.
- Don't discuss an item with another rater before you both label it —
  independent judgements are what makes agreement measurable.
- Skipping is fine and better than guessing. "Not sure" is also a real answer.
- Every label is credited to you by name on the page.
